## 1. (U) Situation

### 1.a. Area of Interest

The area of interest extends 50 km beyond the AO boundary, encompassing HIGHWAY 22 eastbound to the BORDER (grid N7234) and all towns and transportation nodes that could resource enemy reinforcement of OBJ HAMMER within 24 hours.

### 1.b. Area of Operations

#### 1.b.(1) Description

AO IRON is bounded by PL STEEL (north), PL BRONZE (south), GRID LINE 23 (west), and HIGHWAY 22 (east). Dominated by rolling mixed forest with cleared agricultural corridors north–south. See Annex C, Appendix 2 (Operation Overlay).

#### 1.b.(2) Terrain (OAKOC)

- **Observation and Fields of Fire:** OBJ HAMMER sits on a 340m ridgeline offering 3–4 km observation east; forest canopy restricts fields of fire to <200m off the ridge.
- **Avenues of Approach:** Two platoon-sized mounted AAs from the south (ROUTE CLUB, ROUTE SPADE); one company-sized dismounted AA along the eastern treeline (ROUTE DIAMOND).
- **Key Terrain:** OBJ HAMMER (MR-342 ridge). Decisive terrain: the eastern spur, grid NK 142 783, which controls observation of ENY fallback positions.
- **Obstacles:** Stream line along PL BRONZE (fordable for dismounts); mined wire obstacle belt templated vicinity NK 138 779 (IPB-confirmed).
- **Cover and Concealment:** Dense coniferous cover throughout the AO; limited cover in the cleared-fields corridor (east-west). Urban cover in VIC ALTENSTADT (NK 115 790).
#### 1.b.(3) Weather

- **Effects on Operations:** NET: weather FAVORS friendly operations across the campaign window. Decisive Phase II (190500–190800Z) executes in a confirmed dry, clear window with 62% NVG-usable illumination and ceilings well above CAS and UAS minimums. ENY Orlan-10 effectiveness reduced during Phase I precip windows. Principal risks: trafficability on ROUTE CLUB post-1800Z 18 APR (mitigate with ASR SPADE); overnight cold exposure in consolidation. No weather-driven branch or abort trigger anticipated.

## 3. (U) Execution

### 3.a. Commander's Intent

#### 3.a.(1) Purpose

Generate an overmatch of combat power on OBJ HAMMER fast enough to preempt enemy counterattack and hand off the objective to follow-on forces with combat power intact.

#### 3.a.(2) Key Tasks

- Seize OBJ HAMMER with one company decisive, one shaping, one reserve.
- Defeat ENY reserve counterattack forward of PL BRONZE.
- Preserve combat power: less than 10% friendly casualties through consolidation.
- Maintain continuous fires and ISR coverage through transition to follow-on force.

#### 3.a.(3) End State

OBJ HAMMER under TF IRON control by 190900Z; ENY defeated in sector; TF postured for follow-on.

#### 3.a.(4) End State Friendly

TF IRON consolidated on OBJ HAMMER, >90% combat power, Class V at 70% basic load.

#### 3.a.(5) End State Enemy

Enemy force on OBJ HAMMER destroyed or forced to withdraw east of HIGHWAY 22.

#### 3.a.(6) End State Terrain

OBJ HAMMER (MR-342 ridge) and eastern spur under friendly control and observation.

#### 3.a.(7) End State Civil

No civilian casualties; ALTENSTADT village and cultural sites unaffected.

### 3.b. Concept of Operations

#### 3.b.(1) Summary

TF IRON executes a three-phase attack: PHASE I (Shaping, 180600Z–190500Z) — reconnaissance and shaping fires; PHASE II (Decisive, 190500Z–190800Z) — dismounted assault on OBJ HAMMER; PHASE III (Consolidate, 190800Z–200600Z) — consolidate and hand off.

#### 3.b.(2) Maneuver

A Co conducts the decisive operation as the main effort with dismounted assault along AA DIAMOND. B Co fixes ENY on OBJ ANVIL. C Co is reserve prepared to reinforce A Co or defeat counterattack. D Co provides direct-fire support from SBF-1 (NK 132 778).

#### 3.b.(3) Decisive Operation

A Co assaults OBJ HAMMER at 190500Z, seizes the ridge and eastern spur, and consolidates.

#### 3.b.(4) Shaping Operations

B Co fixes the ENY platoon on OBJ ANVIL; SAPPER 2 breaches the wire obstacle belt at NK 138 779 NLT 190430Z; STEEL 6 delivers 12-minute artillery preparation on OBJ HAMMER 190448Z–190500Z.

#### 3.b.(5) Sustaining Operations

Combat Trains (CTCP) established vicinity NK 118 772 by 190400Z. Ambulance exchange point (AXP) at NK 125 776. CL III/V resupply by LMTV convoy from BSA every 8 hrs.

#### 3.b.(6) Reserve

C Co is reserve, held at AA BRONZE, prepared to reinforce A Co (PRIORITY) or block enemy counterattack along ROUTE DIAMOND.

#### 3.b.(7) Fires

See Annex D. Priority of fires to A Co during Phase II.

### Annex A — Task Organization
Task Force IRON is organized around three maneuver companies, a weapons company, and task-organized enablers to seize OBJ HAMMER NLT 190500ZAPR26. A Co (ABLE 6) is the decisive operation and main effort throughout Phase II, task-organized with 2d Plt/A/54 BEB (SAPPER 2) OPCON from 181800ZAPR26 for mobility and breach. B Co (BRAVO 6) is a shaping effort, attacks to fix enemy forces on OBJ ANVIL from 190430ZAPR26, and transitions to supporting effort for consolidation. C Co (CHARLIE 6) is the battalion reserve, postured vic. AA EAGLE NK 112 768, prepared to reinforce A Co, conduct counterattack against enemy mechanized reinforcement from the north, or assume A Co's mission on order.

D Co Weapons (DOG 6) provides direct-fire overwatch from SBF 1 (NK 135 776) and SBF 2 (NK 139 777) with TOW and .50 cal sections, priority to A Co Phase I–II then C Co Phase III. Btry B/4-319 AFAR (STEEL 6) is direct support to 2-503 IN from 181200ZAPR26 through 200600ZAPR26; priority of fires shifts in accordance with Annex D. Scout Plt operates TACON to 173 RSTA SQDN through 190430ZAPR26, then chops back to 2-503 IN control as the battalion's organic recon asset in zone.

### Annex N — Space Operations
Space-based enablers are critical to IRON STRIKE. GPS PNT supports all dismounted and mounted navigation; DAGRs are issued to squad leader and higher. SATCOM (MUOS narrowband, TACSAT) provides BN-to-BCT reachback as the secondary communications path. ISR tipping from national and theater space assets flows through the BCT ACE to BN S-2 within 30 minutes of collection. No offensive space effects are planned or authorized.

The space threat assessment is LOW for IRON STRIKE; however, enemy doctrine includes GPS spoofing and jamming, and the Grafenwoehr training area has historically experienced GPS interference during large exercises. Units carry out pre-mission PNT checks, report any GPS anomaly (drift, sudden fix loss, impossible position) through JSIR on BN O/I net within 15 minutes, and fall back to terrain association and map-and-compass when GPS is degraded.

The BN space officer (dual-hatted with S-6) maintains the running space estimate, submits space support requests through the BCT space support element, and integrates space considerations into the targeting and fires cycle. SATCOM outages, GPS anomalies, and tipping delays are reported immediately to the BN CDR.

**Appendix 2 (N) — Friendly Space Order of Battle**
